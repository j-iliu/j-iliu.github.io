Project 1: Colorizing the Prokudin-Gorskii Photo Collection

CS180 · Intro to Computer Vision and Computational Photography

Part 1 — Single-Scale Alignment

Approach

I used the skeleton code to import, read, and divide the image into its color channels. I then cropped out the margins / edges that were outside of the photograph because they were messing with my correlation metrics. I used the L2 norm metric to sweep from -15 to 15 in both x and y next, getting those numbers from the project information page. I used a double for loop and saved the pair with the lowest L2 error. I used np.roll so I wouldn't have to worry about the size of the images or padding from the shifts. After that, I followed the skeleton code, aligning green and red to blue, then applying the shifts and stacking them.

Part 2 — Multi-Scale Pyramid Alignment

Approach

I used the same starting code as the skeleton to read and divide the image. Using the code from the single alignment as a base, I recursively implemented pyramid alignment, downsampling by 2x each level with sk.transform.rescale. The recursion stopped if the height or width was less than 10 or 20 respectively. I chose such small numbers because I chose a very small guess range of 3 compared to the 15 of the single alignment. I started at 20, and worked my way down to 15, 10, 7, 3, and 1. I found 1 to be too small and 3 to be perfect, being more efficient while still being accurate. I found the rollover to impact the quality of the alignments as well, so in addition to cropping out the edges framing the photograph, I cropped more to cut out any potential information from the rollover. I also tried Normalized Cross Correlation loss instead of L2 loss, and I found that NCC loss works a lot better, so I ended up using that. In the photograph of the emir, the red was extremely misaligned with the blue, so I tried aligning that to the green instead and adding the offset from blue to green to the offset of green to red to get the offset of blue to red, and it worked really well.
