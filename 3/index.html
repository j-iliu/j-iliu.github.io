1.2 — Classical Denoising

Blur strength chosen

The sigmas 8.0 at t=0.25, 4.0 at t=0.5, 2.0 at t=0.75 gave me the best denoised version. Blur works less well at high noise because in that regime blur there, only smidgens of details are left by the noising process and the blur just averages what's left away. In the example of t=0.25, almost all the facial details have been removed by blur, compared to regular noising that at least kept some of them.

1.3 — One-Step Denoising

Observations

At low t, like t=0.25, one step gives a very smooth results. There are no textures anywhere. Texture starts slowly coming back as you raise t, and at 0.75, the textures are almost all back. One step inaccurate because at low t, all the model can do is predict an average shirt, it has no chance to adjust and produce the creases and textures that one would see in a specific shirt.

1.2.3 — Denoising Pure Noise

Patterns observed in the outputs

All outputs for both cases are the same blurry smudge, kind of like all the digits overlaid into one.

Relationship to the training images

The output looks like an average of all the training images.

Why this happens

MSE loss promotes this by rewarding the model is estimating the average of all these digits, the loss is distance and it is minimized if the prediction is smallest distance it can be from the all the ground truths, resulting in averages.

Without the learning rate scheduler

What I did to compensate for removing the scheduler

To compensate, I lowered the learning rate from 1e-2 to a constant 1e-3, so the model takes smaller steps from the start and approaches the same small step size that the scheduler eventually decays to. Without the learning rate scheduler and training to epoch 10, the images are slightly more blobby. The shapes of the numbers are less defined, and it seems like it will take a little more epochs to converge. What you get from this is an easier way to train for more epochs, because the learning rate just stays constant, though you WILL have to train for more epochs.
